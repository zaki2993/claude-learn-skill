How do you find a missing number?

Work backwards from a total to the one number nobody told you.

15 min Needs: adding, taking away, dividing 🧭 Normal

A receipt with a hole in it

A taxi app charges a $3 start fee, then $2 for every kilometre. Your ride cost $17.

The app forgot to show the distance. How far did you go?

Take a guess. No working needed.

  • 7 km
  • 8.5 km
  • 10 km

It's 7 km. 8.5 is what you get if you forget the start fee. 10 is what you get if you add the fee instead of taking it away. So how do you land on 7 every time, without guessing?

So how do you go backwards from a total to the number that's missing?

Cut the receipt into pieces

Let's not use any math words yet. Just draw the receipt as a bar, and cut it up.

Here's the whole receipt: $17. Somewhere inside it is the start fee, and the money for every kilometre.

The first $3 is the start fee. You pay it even for zero kilometres. Cut it off. Everything left, $14, is kilometre money.

Each kilometre is a $2 piece. How many $2 pieces fit in $14? Count them: 7. You went 7 km.

Now the other way round. Drag the distance and watch the fare grow.

Fare: $7

Across: kilometres. Up: dollars. The rising line is the fare, the flat line is $17. Slide until the dot sits on the flat line.

To find the missing number, undo what happened to it, in reverse order: the last thing added comes off first.

Drawing works for a taxi. But what about messy numbers like 1.8 or 0.15? How do you write this down so it works for any numbers?

What people call it

You already did everything. Here are the names for what you saw.

What you sawWhat it's called
The distance nobody told youvariable (usually written \( x \))
The sentence "fee plus kilometre money makes the receipt"equation
Undoing step by step until only the missing number is leftsolving the equation

So how do you write the taxi as an equation, and solve it with any numbers?

Now with numbers

Write the receipt sentence, with \( x \) for the distance:

$$ 3 + 2x = 17 $$

Read it out loud: three dollars, plus two dollars for each of \( x \) kilometres, makes seventeen.

Both sides are the same amount of money. So an equation is a balance: do the same thing to both sides, and it stays true.

  1. Take 3 away from both sides: \( 2x = 14 \) why: the fee was added last, so it comes off first. Doing it to both sides keeps the balance.
  2. Divide both sides by 2: \( x = 7 \) why: \( 2x \) means "2 times \( x \)". Dividing by 2 undoes it.
  3. Check: \( 3 + 2 \times 7 = 17 \) why: put the answer back in. If both sides match, it's right.
Wrong: one side only\( 3 + 2x = 17 \) becomes \( 2x = 17 \), so \( x = 8.5 \). The 3 left the left side, but the right side didn't change. The balance tipped.
Right: both sides\( 3 + 2x - 3 = 17 - 3 \), so \( 2x = 14 \) and \( x = 7 \). Same change on each side, still balanced.
🪤 Classic trap Dividing first. If you divide by 2 first, you must divide everything, the 3 too: \( 1.5 + x = 8.5 \). People write \( 17 \div 2 - 3 = 5.5 \) instead. Take the plain number off first and the trap never opens.

Does "undo in reverse" work on equations from other places too?

Examples

Example 1: A download that's partly done easy

A game update is 12 GB. 5 GB is already downloaded. How much is left? \( x + 5 = 12 \)

  1. Take 5 away from both sides: \( x = 7 \) why: 5 was added to \( x \), so taking 5 away undoes it.
  2. Check: \( 7 + 5 = 12 \) why: both sides match, so 7 GB is left.
Example 2: A draining phone battery medium

Your phone was at 100% and loses 3% for each hour of video. It's at 64% now. How many hours? \( 100 - 3h = 64 \)

  1. Take 100 away from both sides: \( -3h = -36 \) why: get the hours part alone on its side.
  2. Divide both sides by \( -3 \): \( h = 12 \) why: undo "times \( -3 \)". A minus divided by a minus is a plus.
  3. Check: \( 100 - 3 \times 12 = 64 \) why: 12 hours of video.
Example 3: Fahrenheit to Celsius medium

A weather app shows 77 °F. Fahrenheit is 1.8 times Celsius, plus 32. What's that in Celsius? \( 1.8C + 32 = 77 \)

  1. Take 32 away from both sides: \( 1.8C = 45 \) why: the 32 was added last, so it comes off first. Same move as the taxi fee.
  2. Divide both sides by 1.8: \( C = 25 \) why: undo "times 1.8". Messy numbers change nothing about the method.
  3. Check: \( 1.8 \times 25 + 32 = 77 \) why: 25 °C, a warm day.
Example 4: When do two phone plans cost the same? harder

Plan A: $20 a month plus $0.10 a minute. Plan B: $5 a month plus $0.25 a minute. At how many minutes do they cost the same? \( 20 + 0.10m = 5 + 0.25m \)

  1. Take 5 away from both sides: \( 15 + 0.10m = 0.25m \) why: gather the plain numbers on one side.
  2. Take \( 0.10m \) away from both sides: \( 15 = 0.15m \) why: \( m \) is on both sides. Remove the smaller part, so \( m \) lives on one side only.
  3. Divide both sides by 0.15: \( m = 100 \) why: undo "times 0.15".
  4. Check: A costs \( 20 + 10 = 30 \), B costs \( 5 + 25 = 30 \) why: equal at 100 minutes. Talk more, and plan A is cheaper.

Four different places, one method. So what about our taxi?

Solving the problem

  1. Write it down: \( 3 + 2x = 17 \) why: $3 fee, $2 per kilometre, $17 total.
  2. Take the fee off both sides: \( 2x = 14 \) why: the last thing added comes off first.
  3. Divide both sides by 2: \( x = 7 \) why: undo "2 times".
  4. Check: \( 3 + 2 \times 7 = 17 \) why: it matches the receipt. You went 7 km.
🏅 You can now find the missing number whenever something is "a start amount, plus a rate times an amount, makes a total": fares, bills, batteries, temperatures.

Take 6 off both sides first.

\( 4x = 24 \), so \( x = 6 \). Check: \( 4 \times 6 + 6 = 30 \).

🥋 Practice What's the best first step for \( 5x - 2 = 18 \)?

  • Divide the \( 5x \) by 5
  • Add 2 to both sides
  • Take 18 off the left side

The "take away 2" happened last, so undo it first: add 2 to both sides. \( 5x = 20 \), so \( x = 4 \).

🛰️ Real-world The taxi app needs this as code. Write solve(start, rate, total) that returns the missing amount. For the taxi, solve(3, 2, 17) is 7.

🕯️ Hint

Same two steps as on paper: take start off the total, then divide by rate.

🗝️ Solution
function solve(start, rate, total) {
  return (total - start) / rate;
}

The brackets matter: take the start off first, then divide. Without them, only start would be divided: the classic trap, in code.

Cheat card

How do you find a missing number?

  • An equation is a balance: do the same thing to both sides and it stays true.
  • Undo in reverse order: the last thing done comes off first.
  • Usually: take the plain number off, then divide by the number in front of \( x \).
  • \( x \) on both sides? Take the smaller \( x \) part off both sides first.
  • Always check: put the answer back in.
\( a + bx = c \;\Rightarrow\; x = \dfrac{c - a}{b} \)
